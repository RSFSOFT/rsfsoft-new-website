The Challenge

Northern Freight Solutions Inc. was a mid-sized Canadian freight company operating 87 trucks with 120 drivers across Ontario and Quebec. Despite 22 years in business and $14M in annual revenue, their entire operation ran on WhatsApp group chats, paper manifests, and a shared Excel spreadsheet that 6 people edited simultaneously. The chaos was costing them — badly.

The company's COO Sandra Kowalski contacted RSF Soft after a particularly damaging week: three high-value shipments were delivered to the wrong addresses due to a WhatsApp miscommunication, resulting in $28,000 in compensation claims. They had looked at off-the-shelf solutions like Samsara and KeepTruckin but the cost would have been $16,560/month and neither integrated with their existing QuickBooks accounting system.

Development Process

Our 9-Month Build Plan

Five overlapping development phases delivering value at every milestone.

1

🔍 Discovery & Architecture (Months 1–2)

We conducted 47 stakeholder interviews across drivers, dispatchers, operations managers and regular clients. This revealed 23 distinct workflows that needed digitalising. We designed a microservices architecture on AWS (EC2 + RDS + S3) to ensure the system could scale from 87 to 500+ trucks without a rebuild. Wireframes were signed off in Week 6.

2

📱 Driver App — React Native (Months 2–5)

Built for iOS and Android simultaneously using React Native. Features: real-time GPS tracking with 30-second refresh, digital manifests replacing paper, photo proof-of-delivery with geostamp, electronic client signatures, offline capability for rural routes with no signal, and a driver messaging system replacing WhatsApp. Onboarded first 20 drivers in Month 5 for beta testing.

3

🖥️ Dispatcher Dashboard — Web App (Months 3–6)

A browser-based control centre showing all 87 trucks on a live map with real-time ETAs. Dispatchers get drag-and-drop route optimisation powered by Google Maps Platform, automated driver assignment based on location and availability, live traffic-adjusted ETA calculations, and an escalation alerts system for delays over 30 minutes.

4

🌐 Client Portal (Months 5–8)

A white-labelled client portal where businesses can log in to track their shipments live, receive automated SMS delivery notifications via Twilio, download digital delivery receipts with photos and signatures, manage their account, and raise support tickets — eliminating 90% of inbound calls to the office.

5

💰 QuickBooks Integration & Analytics (Months 7–9)

Full two-way QuickBooks API integration: invoices auto-generated on delivery confirmation, payments reconciled automatically, route profitability reports generated weekly. Custom analytics dashboard showing on-time delivery rate, fuel cost per route, driver performance scores, and monthly revenue trends. Went fully live Month 9.

Key Lessons From This Project

The single most important investment we made on this project was the 47 stakeholder interviews in Month 1. Logistics companies have deeply ingrained workflows that have evolved over decades — understanding exactly why people did things the way they did was critical to building software they would actually adopt. Several drivers initially resisted the change; by Month 3, they were the app's biggest advocates because it genuinely made their day easier.

The offline capability was a feature we almost cut to save budget. It turned out to be the most-praised feature of the entire app — 34 of the 87 drivers regularly operate in rural Ontario where cellular coverage is unreliable. Building offline-first and syncing on reconnection was non-negotiable for real-world freight operations. Never sacrifice core usability for cost savings.
