The Problem

11 Stores, 12,400 SKUs and an Online Store That Nobody Could Use

Threads & Trends is one of Lahore’s established mid-market fashion retail chains, operating 11 physical stores across Lahore, Karachi and Islamabad. Founded in 2009, the brand had built strong walk-in traffic and loyal in-store customers. Their core product range covers women’s prêt, men’s formal wear and children’s clothing — over 12,400 active SKUs at any given time across seasonal and permanent collections.

The problem was their digital presence. The existing WooCommerce website was slow (4.8-second load time on mobile), had a disastrous mobile checkout experience and listed only 340 of their 12,400+ products because there was no system to sync inventory from their physical store POS to the website. Online sales generated PKR 8M in the 12 months before the engagement — just 3.1% of total company revenue — from a market where Pakistani e-commerce was growing at 37% year-over-year.

The deeper problem was inventory chaos. Their 11 stores each operated independently, with a different POS system (a mix of QuickBooks POS, Hisaab and manual registers). When an item sold in Lahore, the website still showed it as available. Online orders were regularly placed for out-of-stock items, leading to a 34% order cancellation rate that was destroying customer trust and generating refund costs that consumed much of the online channel’s nominal margin.

The Problems We Were Solving For

No unified inventory across 11 stores: Each store managed its own inventory in isolation. There was no central inventory view — meaning the business had no idea of its total stock position at any given moment. Overstocking in Karachi and understocking in Lahore were discovered by phone call, not by any system. Dead stock sat unseen in back rooms while the website showed items as unavailable.
The WooCommerce site was technically obsolete: A 4.8-second mobile load time was catastrophic in a market where 91% of Pakistani e-commerce traffic is mobile-first. The checkout flow required 11 steps on mobile (industry best practice is 3–4). The payment gateway had a 23% failure rate on Easypaisa — Pakistan’s most popular mobile wallet — because the integration was misconfigured. Every payment failure was a permanently lost customer.
No WhatsApp ordering system despite clear demand: Analysis of their Instagram DMs and Facebook page inbox revealed over 800 product enquiries per month that never converted — customers asking “is this available?” or “can I order via WhatsApp?” with no structured system to handle them. In Pakistan’s retail market, WhatsApp commerce is a significant channel that the business was leaving completely unmonetised.
Zero customer retention infrastructure: The business had no loyalty programme, no email marketing capability, no abandoned cart recovery and no mechanism to re-engage past customers with new arrivals. Customer lifetime value was being destroyed by treating every purchase as a transactional, one-off event. In fashion retail, repeat purchase rate is the primary driver of profitability.
No analytics — decisions made by intuition: The management team had no data on which products were bestsellers online vs. in-store, which cities drove the most online orders, or which marketing campaigns actually drove purchases vs. clicks. A PKR 800,000/month marketing budget was allocated based on the owner’s instinct rather than any attribution data.
The Solution

A Fully Unified Retail Commerce Platform

We didn’t fix the WooCommerce site. We replaced the entire digital infrastructure with a purpose-built, mobile-first platform connected to every store in real time.

Month 1

🔎 Audit & Architecture Design

We conducted a full technical audit of all 11 store POS systems, identified which had API access (4 did), which needed middleware bridges (5) and which needed replacement (2). We designed a central inventory database architecture that would serve as the single source of truth for all stock, regardless of which store it was physically located in. This architecture decision — centralised inventory first — was the foundation everything else was built on.

Month 2–4

📱 New Mobile-First E-Commerce Site (Next.js)

The new e-commerce website was built in Next.js for sub-1-second page loads, with a mobile-first checkout redesigned from 11 steps to 3. All 12,400 SKUs were imported and categorised via a custom admin bulk-upload tool. Product photography was managed via a structured shoot schedule with the client’s in-house photographer. The checkout integrated JazzCash, Easypaisa and credit/debit cards natively — with a fallback to Cash on Delivery which accounts for 67% of Pakistani e-commerce transactions. Payment failure rate dropped from 23% to 1.4%.

Month 2–5

📦 Central Inventory Management System

A custom inventory management system (IMS) became the backbone of the entire platform. Real-time stock levels sync across all 11 stores every 60 seconds via POS API integrations and middleware agents deployed at stores without native API support. When any item sells — in-store or online — stock is decremented across the entire network instantly. Low-stock alerts fire to the warehouse team at configurable thresholds per SKU. The 34% online cancellation rate caused by overselling dropped to under 2% within the first 30 days of go-live.

Month 4–6

💬 WhatsApp Commerce Integration

We built a WhatsApp Business API integration that transforms enquiries into a structured order flow. When a customer messages the brand’s WhatsApp number asking about a product, an automated response sends product images, pricing, sizes and a direct purchase link. A customer service agent dashboard in the admin panel shows all active WhatsApp conversations with full order history and can convert any conversation to a manual order with a single click. WhatsApp became the second-largest revenue channel within 60 days of launch.

Month 5–6

❤ Loyalty Programme & CRM

A points-based loyalty programme rewards every purchase (online and in-store) with redeemable points. Customer accounts now store complete cross-channel purchase history. Automated Klaviyo email flows handle: welcome series, first-purchase follow-up, win-back (30/60/90-day inactive triggers), birthday discounts, new arrival announcements segmented by past purchase category, and abandoned cart recovery sequences. Within 6 months, the email channel alone was generating PKR 2.1M/month in revenue from a list that didn’t exist before the project.

Technical Stack

Technology That Drives Commerce

Every technology choice was driven by the specific requirements of the Pakistani retail market — mobile-first audiences, local payment gateways and WhatsApp-dominant consumer behaviour.

⚡
Next.js (Mobile-First Frontend)
The e-commerce frontend was built in Next.js with server-side rendering for sub-1-second page loads on 3G connections — the typical network condition for Pakistani mobile users. Image optimisation via Next.js Image automatically serves WebP format with lazy loading, reducing the average page weight from 4.2MB (old WooCommerce site) to 380KB. Core Web Vitals scores all green within 2 weeks of launch.
⚙
Laravel + Central IMS API
The inventory management system core was built in Laravel PHP with a PostgreSQL database. RESTful APIs expose stock level data to the e-commerce site, the admin panel, the WhatsApp bot and the POS integration layer. A job queue system (Laravel Horizon) processes 11 simultaneous POS sync streams every 60 seconds without performance impact — even during peak sales periods like Eid and Black Friday.
💬
WhatsApp Business API
The WhatsApp Business API integration includes a product catalogue synced from the IMS, an automated order initiation flow for inbound enquiries and a live agent handover system for complex queries. All WhatsApp orders feed directly into the same order management system as website orders — with the same fulfilment, packing and dispatch workflow. This channel generated PKR 6.9M in revenue in Month 12 alone.
💲
JazzCash + Easypaisa + Stripe
Native payment gateway integrations for JazzCash (Pakistan’s largest mobile wallet by active users), Easypaisa and Stripe for international card payments. Each gateway integration was tested exhaustively across 200+ transaction scenarios before launch. Automatic retry logic handles temporary gateway failures transparently, recovering an estimated 8% of transactions that previously failed permanently on the old WooCommerce site.
💌
Klaviyo Email & SMS
Klaviyo powers the entire retention marketing programme: 14 automated email flows, SMS campaigns for new arrivals, and a loyalty points notification system. The abandoned cart sequence alone — three emails over 48 hours with a 10% discount in the third email — recovers an average of PKR 1.4M per month in revenue that would otherwise be permanently lost. All flows are segmented by gender, past purchase category and city for personalised relevance.
📈
Google Analytics 4 + Metabase
Full GA4 implementation with enhanced e-commerce tracking provides granular data on every step of the purchase funnel. Metabase provides the operations team with a plain-language analytics dashboard: daily revenue by channel and city, bestseller rankings, inventory velocity by SKU and dead stock alerts. For the first time, management makes marketing and buying decisions backed by actual data — not intuition.

Key Learnings

What This Project Proved

01
Inventory Accuracy Is the Foundation of E-Commerce
Every other improvement — better UI, faster load times, more payment options — is undermined if the inventory data is unreliable. The 34% cancellation rate was destroying the online channel before any customer retention work could succeed. Fixing inventory accuracy first, before optimising the funnel, was the correct sequencing decision and explains why the results were so dramatic in the first 30 days.
02
WhatsApp Commerce Is Real in Emerging Markets
The 800 monthly unanswered WhatsApp enquiries we found in the initial audit represented PKR 6.9M in monthly revenue by Month 12. In markets where WhatsApp is the primary communication platform — Pakistan, Nigeria, India, Brazil — treating it as a customer service tool rather than a sales channel is a costly mistake. Structured WhatsApp commerce should be a core feature for any APAC or MENA e-commerce build.
03
Payment Gateway Selection Is a Revenue Decision
The 23% Easypaisa payment failure rate on the old site was invisibly destroying the online channel. In Pakistan, where mobile wallets account for over 40% of e-commerce transactions, a broken gateway integration is not a technical inconvenience — it is a revenue cap. Thorough payment gateway testing, covering all failure modes and edge cases before launch, is non-negotiable for any Pakistani e-commerce build.
04
Email Lists Are Compounding Assets
The email programme generated PKR 2.1M/month from zero — from a list that didn’t exist 12 months earlier. Unlike paid advertising where revenue stops the moment spend stops, an email list is a compounding asset that generates higher returns over time as the list grows and segmentation improves. Every retail business should be building this asset from day one, not as an afterthought when other channels become expensive.
